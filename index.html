Reading Response: Garden of Forking Paths

Date: May 7

I found this to be a very interesting read. It touches on many themes relavent in popular culture. For example, the idea of multiple universes, springing from a decision point, has been portrayed in the MCU and other sci-fi media. I found it notable that the author discussed this brancing paths theory as an effect of time, not space. As far as I am aware, our current understanding of the universe attempts to unify space and time into an aptly named "spacetime". I wonder how this understanding would further the theory of branching paths. Last year I read The Order of Time by Carlo Rovelli, which explored our understanding of time from ancient history up to present day. I finished that book with the conclusion that this is a very incredibly complex topic, but still one that is facinating to try to understand.

Reading Response: Glitch

  • I thought the idea of glitch as a perception of the audience was interesting. Going into this reading, I would have defined glitch as an accident. But is it not a glitch if deliberatly placed there by the artist for the audience? Instead of an accident, it is a break in the expected flow from technology.
  • The instability of glitches over time - what was once a glitch is no longer a glitch
  • Calling glitch the "fingerprints of imperfections", either negative or not, is relevent in the advent of AI art and the conversation about what does a human add to art?
  • The idea of a glitch opening up the technology to the viewer - I find that as time progresses people have a much less sound grasp on how the tech they use day to day actually works. Thinking of glitch as a way to disrupt this, and in turn disrupt capitlisit ideas of planned obsolenscence, ever-increasing profits, etc

    Reading Response: The Aesthetic of Failure

  • different aspects of tech (monitors, browsers, connection speeds) make differing views in the audience
  • Hal Foster resisted the idea of aesthetic tastes being universal
  • the aesthetics of web culture are just a matter of taste if they are not connected with political intent
  • attributes of net art: "collaboration, interactivity, formalism, and reflexivity"
  • net art turns many standards of the art world upside down, such as authentication
  • there is a cultural fear/expectation of tech failng
  • Judith Butler's concept of repition as a way to dismantle cultural concepts
